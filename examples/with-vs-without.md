## Without the skill (score 0)

**No, 6 weeks is probably too high.** For one developer using Claude Code who already has auth, an org/team model and an email provider, I'd expect about **5–8 working days**, or **1.5–2 weeks** once review and QA are included.

## Rough breakdown

| # | Piece | Estimate | What actually takes the time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Invites API | 1–1.5 days | Create, list, revoke and resend. Store invite tokens hashed, with an expiry. Check who's allowed to invite. Handle a duplicate invite or inviting someone who's already a member. |
| 2 | Invite email | 0.5 day | Template plus send. Takes **1–3 days** if you still need to set up an email provider and the DNS records that keep mail out of spam (SPF/DKIM), because DNS changes take time to apply. |
| 3 | Settings screen | 1–1.5 days | Members list, pending invites, invite form, revoke and resend, empty and error states. |
| 4 | Accept-invite page | 1–1.5 days | **The hardest UX part.** The user may be logged out, have no account yet, or be logged in as a different email. The invite may be expired, revoked or already used. |
| 5 | Seat limit on accept | 0.5–1 day | Doing the check plus the insert in one transaction or with a lock, so two people accepting at once can't both take the last seat. You also need to decide whether to warn at invite time too. |

## When 6 weeks would be realistic

- You don't have an org/team or roles model yet, so you'd be building multi-tenancy first.
- Seats are tied to billing, so a seat change means updating the Stripe subscription quantity, handling proration, and limiting what downgraded accounts can do.
- There are heavy process costs: design review, security review, a staged rollout, or a team that's mostly busy with other work.
- The estimate includes buffer for everything else on your plate, not just this feature.

AI estimates often fall back on older figures for a team without AI tools, which is likely where the 6 weeks came from. Coding is the fast part with Claude Code. The real time goes to the accept-flow edge cases and the seat-limit race condition, and that's where I'd spend your testing effort.
